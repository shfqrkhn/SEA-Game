## Finding and priority

The complaint is supported. Recovery has improved recognizable architecture, but remains a clean schematic vehicle. PRO-D remains a broad plain shell with a large unarticulated door. Lighting alone will not produce the concept's formed industrial assemblies.

1. **Construction/silhouette is the primary gap.** Recovery's roof, lower cab, stowage, bumper and boom have large uniform planes, with many fittings visually separate from the structural transitions. The concept uses layered sheet construction, substantial fold returns, seams with depth, shaped support structures, wheel clearances and reinforced articulations. The runtime cab is considerably shallower and shorter in visual proportion. PRO-D's door outer face is plain; the concept shows layered reinforced aperture/door construction and substantial mounting structure. Prioritize broad formed surfaces, complete mounting load paths and believable joint clearances over adding repeated tiny bolts. Evaluate front/side/rear silhouettes before fine hardware.

2. **Surface treatment is generic and inconsistent in physical scale.** `realism.mjs:6–12` supplies one 64×64 grayscale noise texture to paint and rubber. It has no coordinated base-color variation, normal detail, cavity map, authored edge response or semantic material-specific UV scale. The same bump texture uses `.006` paint and `.012` rubber coefficients in a metres-based scene; these are nominal scales, not proof of actual displacement. Normalized box UVs and shape/extrusion UVs differ, so the repeat value cannot establish a shared physical grain size. `face()` at line 60 builds only positions/normals: these polygon surfaces do not have meaningful UVs for the mapped material. A deterministic material-aware UV/world-space finish with restrained fine grain would be more coherent. Keep manufactured paint clean, but distinguish pressed metal, cast metal, machined faces, rubber and woven upholstery using construction-appropriate response. The generated concept's wear is optional; it is not the source of realism by itself.

3. **Contact/depth definition is weak at small assembly scale.** `game-scene.mjs:20` gives every subject a 24 m-wide shadow camera at 2048 pixels, approximately 11.7 mm per texel before filtering. A crew cell occupies only a small fraction of that shadow map. The `.015` normal bias is also substantial relative to small fittings. The saved Recovery has a broad hard-looking diagonal cast silhouette despite the light-studio comment, while small recesses and mounts lack the concept's contact separation. There is no AO map/contact-occlusion mechanism. Avoid indiscriminately darkening everything: that disguises the geometry rather than repairing it.

4. **Transparent glazing is not a glass qualification.** Recovery's pale `.28` opacity pane reveals the cabin, but it reads nearly absent against the bright environment. PRO's darker `.58` panes conceal interiors in the saved side view. Both are opacity approximations with depth writing disabled, rather than actual layered reflective/transmissive glazing. Check assembled front and grazing views for readable seals, reflection, tint, thickness and interior visibility, and assess sorting with other panes. Do not introduce transmission blindly into the shared scene/UI renderer without testing its render-target interaction and GPU budget.

5. **View matching is missing.** The saved runtime uses a high three-quarter game camera. Recovery's concept hero is lower and closer; PRO-D's concept exposes front-facing seat cushions, while the real runtime rear boarding view correctly sees seat backs. Current screenshot comparisons therefore do not establish a matched-angle verdict for individual details. Keep forward seat orientation and canonical counts. Add fixed lower side/front/rear comparison poses and model-only crops at the same approximate subject size; preserve the normal game camera separately.
