# Recovery concept/render gap review

Date: 2026-10-09. Scope: visual comparison of the saved original concept `docs/game-design/concepts/recovery-assemblies-v1.png` and actual candidate screenshot `recovery-3746154-assembled.png`. This is a shared-context adversarial review, not independent acceptance, dimensional verification or manufacturer certification. No code or browser was changed for this review.

## Finding

The actual model communicates an eight-wheel recovery vehicle, front cab and rear crane, but remains visibly a simplified block assembly. It does not yet match the concept's detailed construction. Adding more small bolts alone will not close the gap: the largest differences concern body proportions, cab/glazing construction, tire shape and crane/mechanical packaging. Visual acceptance remains OPEN.

The concept and screenshot have different elevations, opposing front-quarter orientations and lighting. Exact length/height ratios, unseen side construction and physical fit cannot be established from this pair. The generated concept is a design direction and can contain invented hardware; it must not become an authority for mechanical dimensions or game rules.

## Priority changes

| Priority / area | Visible gap | Next implementation change and verification |
|---|---|---|
| P1 cab and silhouette | The rendered cab reads as a large uninterrupted wedge with a broad flat roof and simple front/side transitions. The concept separates windshield surround, door, lower grille housing, roof edge and cab skirt with meaningful joints and shaped surfaces. | Establish a consistent matched front-quarter and side camera pair first. Reshape the main cab panels with deliberate edge transitions; separate the roof lip, windshield surround, door perimeter and lower front housing before adding fine detail. Compare silhouette overlays without asserting unverified dimensions. |
| P1 glazing and interior | The rendered windshield is dark, sharply angular and nearly flat in appearance; the side pane and door structure have little visible depth. The concept has recessed glazing, seals, distinct frames, visible interior depth and proportionate wipers. | Recess panes within shaped frames, add continuous rubber seals and realistic pane thickness, model a readable dashboard/steering/seat silhouette behind glass, and keep wipers attached to the appropriate glass plane. Inspect assembled/cutaway views with several lighting angles. |
| P1 tires, rims and wheel guards | Tires read as narrow black polygonal rings; fine tread is largely absent at this screenshot scale. Hubs are simplified flat discs, and guards have sharp, block-like transitions. Concept tires have rounded sidewalls, substantial tread, rim recesses and integrated hub/fastener detail. | Make tire cross-sections rounded with shoulder/sidewall transitions, add repeating tread with plausible spacing, deepen rim/hub recesses, separate dark rubber from painted metal, and shape guards around wheel clearance. Preserve canonical eight wheels/four axles. Inspect wheel close-ups and full-vehicle scale, not just geometry counts. |
| P1 crane and recovery equipment | The rendered boom is a mostly rectangular beam with sparse exposed mechanism; its hooked cable is small and simplified. The concept presents a connected pivot, lift cylinder, boom sections, routing, sheaves, hook and visible winch apparatus. | Build readable boom/pivot/cylinder relationships with cylindrical pins, braced mounts, consistent cable paths and attached sheaves; add a recognizable hook and compact connected winch/fairlead assembly. Verify assembled and exploded relationships rather than isolated decorative pieces. |
| P1 body and mechanical packaging | The actual rear service body has box panels but little visible underbody equipment, cab-adjacent exhaust/intake or distinct winch packaging. The concept's chassis, lockers, cab services and recovery equipment form a coherent system. | Establish visible frame rails, axle/differential/suspension groups and underbody clearance; add cab-adjacent service towers and connected rear recovery hardware where references support them. Differentiate locker doors, seams, hinges/latches and guarded service points. Avoid clutter that obscures silhouette or floats away from mounting surfaces. |
| P2 materials and lighting | Actual painted panels have largely uniform olive shading, rubber looks flat black, and glass is almost opaque. The cast shadow is visibly hard-edged. The concept distinguishes painted metal, rubber, glass, cable and exposed machined surfaces with softer directional illumination. | Use coherent per-material roughness/color variation, restrained seams and subtle surface normals; improve glass reflections/interior visibility and metal/rubber separation. Tune directional/ambient lighting and shadow softness. Test fixed-light turntable rotation and contact; this single image does not prove the shadow is stale or responsive. Disable shadows if that behavior cannot be made credible. |
| P2 front hardware and finishing | Lamps, bumper, tow fittings and mirror are recognizable but still read as separate primitives with abrupt surface changes. The concept integrates bezels, mounts, connectors and guards. | Shape lamp housings and lenses, bumper mounts, mirror brackets and tow hardware as connected assemblies. Add only details that remain useful at normal game scale or are exposed during inspection. |

## Required next evidence

Produce matched side/front-quarter renders plus cab, wheel, crane and winch close-ups at recorded camera/lighting settings. Review component attachment and visibility in assembled, exploded and cutaway views. Recheck wheel/seat topology and all-vertex camera bounds after geometry edits, then both-role scene interaction, resource disposal and low-end performance at applicable scale. Preserve generated concept provenance and explicitly reject any concept detail contradicted by canonical topology or inspected primary references.

A model-test pass or increased primitive count cannot close this review. Closure requires fresh actual renders that demonstrate improvement against the visible gaps above, without sacrificing embedded-runtime limits, coherent materials or gameplay usability.
